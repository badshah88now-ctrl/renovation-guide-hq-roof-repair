Roof Repair Cost in 2026: Shingle Replacement, Leak Fixes, and When to Replace

In 2026, the average U.S. roof repair costs between $400 and $2,000, with most homeowners paying around $1,176 for a professional fix. Minor repairs — replacing a few blown-off shingles or resealing a pipe boot — run $150 to $500. Moderate repairs like chimney re-flashing or valley fixes land between $500 and $1,500. Major structural repairs involving rotted decking or sagging can reach $1,500 to $5,000 or more. A full roof replacement, by comparison, averages about $9,611 nationally. The right move almost always comes down to three questions: how old is your roof, how widespread is the damage, and what does the repair cost relative to a replacement?

A single missing shingle is a quick, cheap fix. A sagging section with rotted decking is a different animal entirely. Leak fixes deserve a special note: finding the leak costs as much as fixing it. Water travels along rafters and decking before it shows inside, so the stain on the ceiling is rarely directly below the failed shingle. A roofer who quotes a leak fix without getting on the roof and tracing it is guessing.

Flashing repairs are small metal with big consequences. Chimney flashing, valley flashing, and pipe boots are where most roof leaks start, and re-flashing a chimney is a moderate repair that prevents a major one. Sagging is the line where repair gets serious: a sag means structure, not surface, and it needs an inspection before anyone talks price.

Three decision rules settle repair versus replace. The 30–50 percent rule: if the repair costs 30 to 50 percent of a replacement, replace. The 25 percent rule: if more than a quarter of the roof is damaged, replace. The age rule: a roof near the end of its rated life is not worth patching twice. And the recurrence test: the same leak fixed twice is a replacement waiting to happen.

Safety first on DIY. A single-story, low-slope shingle swap is reasonable for a careful homeowner with the right ladder and a dry day. Anything steep, two-story, or structural is pro work — falls from roofs are one of the most common serious home injuries. Before you file an insurance claim, document everything with photos and get a roofer's written assessment; storm-chasers who knock after a storm and offer to handle the claim for you are a red flag, not a convenience.
